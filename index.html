01 / THE FIELD

Behind every reliable
digital experience

System administrators help organizations keep their technology running. They maintain operating systems, manage user access, troubleshoot issues, protect data, and support the infrastructure people depend on every day.

↘

02 / CAREER ROADMAP

Every expert starts
somewhere.

Career progression can vary by organization. These levels show one common route, with experience and continued learning helping you move forward.

01
ENTRY LEVELBuild your foundation

IT Support / Help Desk Technician

Provides first-line technical assistance and helps users resolve common hardware, software, account, and connectivity problems.

Key responsibilities

  • Respond to support requests and document solutions
  • Set up computers, accounts, and peripherals
  • Diagnose basic software and network issues

Skills to develop

  • Customer communication and troubleshooting
  • Windows basics and account management
  • Basic networking and ticket documentation
FOCUS: SOLVE & SUPPORT↗
02
INTERMEDIATEManage daily operations

System Administrator

Maintains an organization's computer systems, servers, user accounts, backups, and security settings to keep services dependable.

Key responsibilities

  • Manage servers, users, permissions, and updates
  • Monitor system health and investigate incidents
  • Maintain backup schedules and recovery procedures

Skills to develop

  • Windows Server or Linux administration
  • Networking, access control, and security
  • Scripting and systematic problem-solving
FOCUS: MAINTAIN & PROTECT↗
03
ADVANCEDDesign and improve systems

Systems Engineer

Plans, builds, integrates, and improves the systems and infrastructure that support an organization's technology services.

Key responsibilities

  • Design server and network infrastructure solutions
  • Automate repeatable deployment and maintenance tasks
  • Improve performance, reliability, and resilience

Skills to develop

  • Advanced networking and virtualization
  • Automation, scripting, and cloud fundamentals
  • Architecture planning and technical documentation
FOCUS: DESIGN & AUTOMATE↗
04
LEADERSHIPGuide teams and strategy

IT Infrastructure Manager

Oversees infrastructure services, technology priorities, team operations, service quality, and long-term planning.

Key responsibilities

  • Coordinate infrastructure projects and technical teams
  • Plan budgets, capacity, service continuity, and upgrades
  • Set operational standards and review service risks

Skills to develop

  • Leadership, communication, and decision-making
  • Project planning and risk management
  • Broad infrastructure knowledge and service strategy
FOCUS: LEAD & PLAN↗

Career note: Titles, requirements, and promotion timelines differ between employers. Certifications, practical projects, and hands-on experience can support progression, but no single route is required.

04 / JOB ROLES

Related roles,
different priorities.

These roles work together, but each one focuses on a different part of the IT environment.

01 / SUPPORT

IT Support Technician

Help users and restore access to working technology.

User support

Common tasks
Ticket handling, device setup, basic troubleshooting.

02 / ADMINISTRATION

System Administrator

Maintain servers, accounts, operating systems, and services.

System health

Common tasks
Updates, permissions, monitoring, backup checks.

03 / ENGINEERING

Systems Engineer

Design and integrate infrastructure solutions.

System design

Common tasks
Architecture, automation, integration, capacity planning.

i

How they work together: Support technicians often identify and escalate recurring problems. System administrators maintain the underlying services, while systems engineers may redesign or automate solutions to prevent future issues.
